Abstract

Multi-fingered dexterous hands possess manipulation potential comparable to that of human hands. However, their high degrees of freedom and complex contact dynamics make teleoperation challenging, particularly in terms of motion retargeting accuracy, lack of sensory feedback, and poor system generalization. To address these issues, we propose a unified vision–tactile fusion teleoperation system for Dexterous hands (VTFTHand) with novel framework and algorithmic designs.

For motion retargeting, we propose an Adaptive Error-Weight Dex-Retargeting (AEWD-Retargeting) algorithm, which employs an adaptive error-weighting to suppress the influence of noise and outliers. For tactile interaction, we design an adaptive tactile grasping algorithm based on Pressure Sensitive Touch (PST) fingertip sensors, achieving closed-loop force-controlled grasping in monocular teleoperation for the first time.

Experimental results show that, compared to baseline, VTFTHand improves grasping success rates by 20% and 50%, reduces task completion times by 20% and 40%, and enhances manipulation efficiency by 36% and 53%. These results validate the superiority of our approach in precision, robustness, and efficiency, while also demonstrating the system's scalability and practical applicability.

Keywords: Dexterous hand teleoperation Vision–tactile fusion Motion retargeting Adaptive tactile grasping

Contributions

1AEWD-Retargeting Algorithm

An adaptive error-weight dex-retargeting algorithm that dynamically adjusts weights based on error magnitude, suppressing contribution of vectors with large errors to the total loss. Under strong noise (σ=2.0), achieves 2–5× higher precision compared to Dex-Retargeting.

2Tactile Adaptive Grasping

A fingertip tactile adaptive PI hybrid force control algorithm integrating preloaded object pose, adaptive pressure fine-tuning, and robust PI control. First closed-loop tactile grasping in monocular vision teleoperation. Success rate: 67.5% → 92.5%.

3VTFTHand System Framework

A unified framework compatible with 6+ dexterous hands (Shadow, Allegro, Inspire, Ability, LEAP, SVH) via URDF interface. Supports Isaac Gym, MuJoCo simulation and real hardware. ROS-based distributed deployment with seamless vision-tactile mode switching.
